# Records Request Drafts — Alameda PD

Two drafts. **Send the informal one first.** Agencies respond better to a constituent describing a project than to a statutory demand, and an informal yes gets you a recurring export that a formal request often can't compel.

Replace `[BRACKETED]` fields before sending.

## Draft A — Informal email (send this first)

**To:** APDRecords@alamedaca.gov
**Subject:** Question about machine-readable crime data exports

> Hello,
>
> My name is [NAME] and I'm a resident of Alameda. A group of us are building a free public website that presents Alameda crime data in a more analyzable form — trends over time, per-capita rates, and comparisons with neighboring cities — for residents trying to understand what's actually happening in town.
>
> We know the department already publishes incident data through the CitizenRIMS portal at alameda.citizenrims.com, and we'd rather work from a clean export than pull data off the map page. Before filing a formal Public Records Act request, I wanted to ask directly:
>
> 1. Does the department have a standard report or export of incident, call-for-service, and arrest data — something already produced from RIMS in CSV, Excel, or similar?
> 2. If so, would it be possible to receive it on a recurring basis (monthly, or whatever cadence is easiest), rather than through repeated individual requests?
> 3. Is there a preferred format or contact for this kind of ongoing data request?
>
> The site will be free, non-commercial, and will clearly state that it is not an official record of the Alameda Police Department. We're glad to share what we build before it goes public, and to work within whatever constraints make sense on your end — including on how individual-level information is handled. We do not intend to publish booking photographs.
>
> Happy to talk by phone if that's easier.
>
> Thank you,
> [NAME]
> [EMAIL] · [PHONE]

## Draft B — Formal CPRA request

File at https://cityofalamedaca.nextrequest.com/requests/new, or email records with this text.

> **Request under the California Public Records Act (Gov. Code § 7920.000 et seq.)**
>
> To the Custodian of Records, Alameda Police Department:
>
> Pursuant to the California Public Records Act, I request copies of the following records. Where records exist in an electronic format, I request them in that electronic format pursuant to Government Code sections 7922.570 and 7922.575 — preferably CSV, XLSX, or JSON. I am not requesting paper copies or PDFs.
>
> **1. Incident / offense reports (data extract, not narratives), from January 1, [YEAR] to the present**, including for each record, to the extent maintained: incident or case number; date and time reported; date and time of occurrence; incident type / offense classification (both the department's internal code and any UCR/NIBRS classification); block-level or intersection-level location; latitude/longitude if maintained; reporting district, beat, or geographic subdivision; disposition and clearance status; and whether the incident was self-initiated or citizen-reported.
>
> **2. Calls for service, same date range**, including: call number; date and time received, dispatched, on-scene, and cleared; call type as received and as cleared; priority; block-level location; and disposition.
>
> **3. Arrest data, same date range**, including: arrest date and time; arrest offense(s) and code section(s); block-level location; whether the arrest was custodial or cite-and-release; and the arrestee's age, sex, and race/ethnicity as recorded for reporting purposes.
>
> **4. Traffic stop data collected under the Racial and Identity Profiling Act (Gov. Code § 12525.5), same date range**, in the format submitted to the California Department of Justice.
>
> **5. Records sufficient to interpret the above**, including any data dictionary, code list, offense-code crosswalk, field definitions, or record layout for the datasets produced.
>
> **6. Any records retention schedule or written policy** governing how long the datasets above are retained and how they are published to the public-facing CitizenRIMS portal.
>
> **I am not requesting** investigative narratives, booking photographs, personal identifying information of victims, witnesses, or reporting parties, or any record or field exempt from disclosure. Where a record contains both disclosable and exempt information, I request the disclosable portions with exempt material redacted, per Government Code section 7922.525.
>
> **Format and recurrence:** If these datasets are produced from the department's records management system as an existing standard report, please provide that report as-is. If an ongoing or recurring production (for example, monthly) is available, I would prefer that to repeated individual requests, and I am glad to discuss whatever arrangement is most workable for the department.
>
> **Fees:** Please notify me before incurring any cost, and provide a written estimate and the statutory basis for any charge beyond the direct cost of duplication under section 7922.575. I authorize costs up to $[AMOUNT] without further approval.
>
> **Assistance:** If any part of this request is unclear, overbroad, or would be substantially easier to fulfill in a different form, please contact me under Government Code section 7922.600 — I would rather adjust the request than have it denied. If any portion is withheld, please state the specific exemption claimed and the basis for it.
>
> I look forward to your response within 10 days as provided by Government Code section 7922.535.
>
> Sincerely,
> [NAME]
> [ADDRESS] · [EMAIL] · [PHONE]

## Practical notes

- **The 10-day clock is a determination deadline, not a production deadline.** The agency must tell you within 10 days whether it has disclosable records; production follows within a "reasonable" time, extendable by 14 days in defined circumstances. Expect weeks, not days.
- **Narrow the date range if the first response cites burden.** Start with 2 years, expand once the pipeline works.
- **Cost-shifting is the likely friction point.** Gov. Code § 7922.575 lets an agency charge for programming and computer services where the request requires data compilation, extraction, or programming. Asking for an *existing standard export* is what keeps you on the direct-duplication-cost side of that line — which is why the language above leads with it.
- **Log everything.** Dates sent, dates responded, fee estimates, exemptions claimed. If the project ever needs to escalate, the paper trail is the case.
- **Keep the informal channel warm regardless.** A cooperative records clerk is worth more than a perfectly drafted request.
